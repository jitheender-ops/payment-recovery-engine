Payment Failure Recovery Engine · Architecture Guide

When a payment fails, this system decides what to do next — carefully.

It figures out whether to retry, when to retry, and on which payment method — using AI for the judgment, but wrapping that AI in hard, non-negotiable safety rules so it can never do something reckless with real money.

1The problem this solves

Failed payments are not rare, and they are not hopeless.

In India, roughly 1 in 8 online payments fails. The money isn't gone — the customer usually still wants the thing. The payment just tripped over something: an OTP screen they abandoned, a bank that was briefly down, a card that hit its daily limit, a UPI request they forgot to approve.

Most shops respond with a dumb fixed schedule: retry the same charge three times, fifteen minutes apart, on the same card. That's wasteful and sometimes rude. Retrying a stolen card or a fraud block is pointless and risky. Retrying a bank that's still down just fails again. And hammering a customer who simply ran out of balance doesn't magically refill their account.

The goal
Replace blind retries with a decision that understands why the payment failed — and then prove, with numbers, that the smarter approach recovers more money with fewer attempts.
+₹11.7L
extra net revenue per ₹1 crore of failed volume, vs. a fixed 3-retry baseline
−15%
fewer retry attempts needed
0%
false retries (retrying things that should never be retried)

These aren't hand-picked numbers — they come from the built-in evaluation harness. More on that below.

2The big idea: AI in a straitjacket

The single most important design decision in the whole system.

An LLM is great at judgment — "this looks like a bank outage, wait an hour" — but you should never let one directly move money. So this system sandwiches the AI between two layers of boring, predictable code:

🔒
Deterministic
(before)
Verify the webhook, dedupe it, classify the error with a lookup table. No AI needed for any of this.
→
🧠
The AI
(constrained)
Picks exactly one of 5 fixed actions. It cannot invent anything new or run anything itself.
→
🛡️
Deterministic
(after)
A guardrail checks the choice against 8 hard rules. Only then does anything touch the payment gateway.

Think of it like a hospital. The AI is a talented junior doctor who can recommend a treatment, but they work from a fixed menu of approved options, and every prescription must be signed off by a strict attending physician before it reaches the patient. The junior doctor never touches the medicine cabinet directly.

Why both sides are deterministic
Before the AI: anything a simple lookup table can solve, a lookup table solves. Calling an LLM to read a structured error code like insufficient_funds is slow, costly, and fragile.
After the AI: the AI can hallucinate. The guardrail is the safety net that catches a bad idea before any money moves.

3Follow one failed payment, end to end

The whole system in a single story.

Priya tries to buy something for ₹2,500 with her card. The bank's OTP page times out and the payment fails. Here's what happens next, step by step:

1 · INGEST
Razorpay sends a webhook
The system first checks the message really is from Razorpay (a cryptographic signature), then checks it hasn't already seen this exact event, then saves it. It replies "got it" in under 10 milliseconds and does the heavy lifting in the background — so Razorpay is never kept waiting.
2 · CLASSIFY
What kind of failure is this?
A lookup table reads the error code and labels it — here, 3ds_dropoff (customer abandoned the OTP step). This is also where fraud and stolen cards get flagged as "never retry" and are quietly abandoned before the AI is even called.
3 · DECIDE
The AI picks an action
The policy agent sees the full context — failure type, amount, time of day, how many times Priya has already been contacted — and chooses one of five actions. For an OTP drop-off it might say: "switch to UPI and nudge her, since UPI has no OTP step."
4 · GUARDRAIL
The safety check
Eight rules inspect the choice: Is it past 11 PM? Has she been contacted too many times? Is the amount over the ceiling? Is there an idempotency key? If any rule fails, the action is blocked and the reason is written down.
5 · MESSAGE
Write a friendly note
If the customer needs to be nudged, a short message (≤160 characters, SMS-sized) is generated — by the AI if it's available, otherwise from a ready-made template. It never blocks the decision.
6 · EXECUTE / SCHEDULE
Act on it
The system creates a Razorpay Payment Link and sends it. Or, if the AI said "wait 4 hours," the attempt is parked and the scheduler fires it later — re-checking all the safety rules at that moment, because Priya might have opted out in the meantime.

When Priya eventually pays the link, a payment.captured webhook comes back. The system matches it to the case, marks the money as recovered, and — crucially — records whether our link earned it or whether she would have paid anyway. That honesty is what makes the headline numbers trustworthy.

4The six layers, in a little more depth

Each layer has one job and hands off to the next.

Layer 1 — Ingestion (src/ingestion/)

The front door. Two kinds of events arrive here: Razorpay webhooks (a payment failed or was captured) and merchant-pushed risk events (more on those in the chasers section). Every event goes through the same three checks:

  • Signature verification — an HMAC-SHA256 check proves the message really came from Razorpay (or the merchant) and wasn't tampered with. An empty or missing secret means everything is rejected — the system fails closed, not open.
  • Idempotency (dedup) — webhooks get delivered more than once. A unique table makes sure the same event is only ever processed once.
  • Append-only event store — the raw event is saved before the system says "OK." If the process crashes a moment later, nothing is lost; a background sweep picks it up.

Layer 2 — Classifier (src/classifier/)

Translates Razorpay's raw error codes into a clean failure taxonomy — about 14 named classes like insufficient_funds, bank_downtime, 3ds_dropoff, fraud_block. Each class carries three yes/no flags: is it retryable? is it a hard decline (never retry)? would switching payment method help? This is pure lookup + rules loaded from a YAML file — deliberately no AI.

Layer 3 — Policy Agent (src/agent/)

The brain. It's handed a FailureContext — everything known about this failure and this customer — and must return one of five fixed actions as structured JSON. Two implementations exist: an LLM agent (Claude or GPT) for real judgment, and an XGBoost model as a fast, cheap fallback. If the LLM is down, times out, or returns nonsense, the system silently falls back to XGBoost and records that it did so — a counter makes the degradation visible instead of pretending the AI answered.

Layer 4 — Guardrail Gate (src/guardrail/)

The strict attending physician. It runs after the AI decides and before anything executes. It validates the output schema and checks 8 business rules. It never short-circuits: it checks all rules and reports all violations, so the audit log shows the complete picture, not just the first problem.

Layer 5 — Messaging (src/messaging/)

Writes the customer-facing nudge. Tries the LLM with a tight 3-second timeout; if that fails it uses a Jinja2 template. It's careful with words: for an abandoned cart it will never say "your payment failed," because no payment was ever attempted. The message rides along as the Payment Link's checkout description so the customer actually sees it.

Layer 6 — Scheduler (src/scheduler.py)

The heartbeat that makes "wait and retry later" actually happen. It's a simple loop inside the same process (no Celery, no message broker) that wakes every 60 seconds and runs eight cleanup sweeps. Without it, deferred retries would be written to the database and simply never fire.

5The only five things the AI can choose

A fixed menu. Anything off-menu is rejected automatically.

ActionWhat it meansTypical use
retry_nowCharge again immediatelyTransient network or gateway glitch
retry_atCharge again at a specific timeBank downtime — wait for it to recover
switch_railRetry on a different payment methodOTP drop-off → suggest UPI; card limit → suggest UPI/netbanking
nudge_customerSend the customer a message with a linkInsufficient funds; UPI request they forgot to approve
abandonGive up — do nothingFraud, stolen card, customer cancelled, or too risky
Why a fixed menu matters
The AI's output is validated by Pydantic against a strict schema. If it invents a sixth action, returns malformed JSON, or adds extra fields, the answer is rejected and the system falls back safely. Freeform output is never accepted — that's the difference between an assistant and a liability.

6What stops it doing something stupid with real money

This is the first question any fintech reviewer asks. Here's the honest answer.

#RuleDefault
1Hard-decline blocklist. Fraud, stolen cards, and permanent declines are caught before the AI is ever called.—
2Max retries per payment. One payment can only be retried so many times.3
3Max retries per customer per 24h. Stops one unlucky person being hammered across many payments.5
4Amount ceiling. Large amounts need explicit consent, not automatic retry.₹50,000
5Consent window. Stop chasing after this long.72 hours
6Nudge rate limit. Max messages per customer per day.2 / day
7Time-of-day blackout. No retries in the middle of the night (bank success rates crater anyway).11 PM – 7 AM IST
8Idempotency key required. Every attempt carries a unique, deterministic key so a replayed webhook can't create a second charge.always

Beyond the eight rules — the deeper safety machinery

🛡️ No short-circuiting
All rules are checked and all violations reported, so the audit trail is complete — not just the first failure.
🔁 Write-ahead logging
The attempt is saved to the database before Razorpay is called. If we crash mid-call, there's a record, and we can't accidentally charge twice.
⏰ Re-check at fire time
A retry scheduled for 4 hours later is re-validated when it actually fires. If the customer opted out at 11 PM, the 2 AM retry is cancelled — not sent.
🌙 Blackout-aware scheduling
A "retry at 11:05 PM" approved at 10:30 PM gets nudged forward to morning, so it doesn't waste its slot dying at fire time.
🧾 Idempotency race fails closed
If two webhooks race to create the same retry, a database UNIQUE constraint picks one winner before any API call. The loser exits cleanly.
📉 Graceful LLM fallback
If the AI provider is down, XGBoost takes over and a counter records it. The system never blocks, and never pretends the AI answered when it didn't.

7Beyond failed payments: the four "chasers"

Not all lost money announces itself with a webhook.

A failed card charge is easy — Razorpay tells you about it. But an abandoned cart, a subscription that stopped renewing, an overdue invoice, or a failed autopay (mandate) only exist in the merchant's own systems. Nobody sends a webhook for those.

So the merchant pushes them to a POST /risks endpoint (also HMAC-signed). The engine opens a "recovery case" and chases it through the exact same AI → guardrail → payment-link pipeline. Each type gets its own sensible limits:

Risk typeMax contactsConsent windowFirst touchSuggested method
Abandoned cart248 hoursafter 1 hourany
Subscription failure47 daysimmediatelyUPI
Overdue invoice430 daysimmediatelyany
Failed mandate / autopay37 daysafter 24 hoursUPI
One pipeline, five doors in
The genius is that these aren't five separate systems. A failed charge, a cold cart, and an overdue invoice are all just "money we might get back." They share one case table, one attempt budget, one set of safety rules, and one recovery flow — they only differ in how they were detected.

8The data: a ledger built for honesty

PostgreSQL tables designed so the headline number can't lie.

The centre of the schema is recovery_cases — one row per unit of money at risk, whatever the source. Everything else hangs off it:

TableWhat it holds
webhook_events / risk_eventsAppend-only log of everything that arrived. Fully replayable.
payment_failuresThe enriched, classified record of a failed payment.
recovery_casesThe star: money at risk, money recovered, attempt budget, and state (open / recovered / exhausted / abandoned / expired / opted_out).
retry_attemptsEvery attempt with its idempotency key, the AI's decision, whether the guardrail passed, and the outcome.
retry_ledgerPer-customer tally for rate limits + consent status (granted / opted out).
promises_to_pay"I'll pay by Friday" — and whether that promise was kept or broken.
case_eventsAppend-only audit trail of everything done to a case. Never edited, never deleted.
The honesty column: recovered_via_attempt_id
When money comes back, the system records which of our attempts earned it. If this field is empty, it means the customer paid on their own — that's still a recovery, but the engine doesn't take credit for it. This one column is what separates "we recovered ₹X" from "we can prove our actions recovered ₹X."

9The heartbeat: eight sweeps every minute

A background loop that makes deferred decisions real and cleans up messes.

The scheduler is an asyncio task living inside the same FastAPI process. Every 60 seconds it runs eight "sweeps." It's deliberately simple — no broker, no second deployment — because at the volume of a single merchant, one indexed poll a minute is plenty.

SweepWhat it does, in plain words
fire_due_retries"Retry in 4 hours" was said 4 hours ago — fire it now (re-checking safety rules first).
reconcile_eventsA webhook was saved but its background task never ran (crash/deploy). Run it now.
reconcile_risk_eventsSame as above, but for merchant-pushed risk events.
reconcile_stale_attemptsAn attempt was written but we never learned the outcome. Resolve it honestly (fail-closed).
cancel_links_for_closed_casesWhen a case finishes, cancel its payment links so they can't be paid late and double-counted.
expire_promisesA promise-to-pay date passed with no money. Mark it broken and hand the case back to the chaser.
chase_due_casesFind cart/subscription/invoice/mandate cases whose wait is over, and chase them.
report_due_casesCount payment-failure cases waiting on their next webhook, so the backlog stays visible.
A dead-man's switch
Every tick stamps a scheduler_heartbeat row. If the scheduler quietly dies, the Operations dashboard notices the heartbeat going stale within a few minutes — instead of nobody realising for days that deferred retries stopped firing.

10The one part customers actually see

A recovery page, reached by a signed link.

When the system nudges a customer, it doesn't send a bare "pay me" link. It sends them to a small recovery page (/recover/<token>) that explains what happened, shows the amount, and offers a way to pay. Three rules hold this page together:

  • The server decides the state. The page never guesses. It reads the real case from the database on every request, so a customer who reloads mid-payment sees "still confirming" — the truth — not a made-up answer.
  • Never offer a second payment while the first might still be alive. A double-tap on a bad connection must not become two charges.
  • Say nothing we don't know. If a status is genuinely unknown, the page admits it and offers a way forward, instead of picking the optimistic reading.

The link itself is a signed, expiring token: unguessable, scoped to exactly one case, valid for about a day, and containing no personal data (because URLs end up in SMS logs and browser history). The page also has a one-click opt-out — the customer's stop button — which immediately closes every open case for them. It supports English and Hindi.

11Proving it works: the eval harness

Almost nobody builds one. This is the loudest signal in the whole project.

The team doesn't claim the AI is better — they measure it. A standalone harness simulates ~5,000 failed payments through four policies (no retry, fixed 3-retry, XGBoost, and the LLM agent) across multiple random seeds, and compares the results.

The one assumption that matters

"A retry is not a fresh payment."
The payment already failed for a reason, so the bank's normal approval rate tells you little. What matters is the chance the blocker cleared:
P(retry succeeds) = P(blocker cleared) × P(payment clears once blocker is gone)
An earlier version ignored this and reported a fantasy ~73% recovery. The corrected model lands in the 15–30% band that real-world figures show — and a test fails if it drifts out.

The result

PolicyRecovery rateFalse retriesNet ₹ per ₹1Cr failed
No retry0%0%₹0
Fixed 3-retry19.2%8.0%₹17.96L
XGBoost / rules30.2%0%₹29.66L

To make sure the difference is real and not luck, the harness uses common random numbers: every policy sees the identical scenarios, so outcomes can be compared one-to-one. The verdict: +11.03 percentage points recovery with a 95% confidence interval that doesn't touch zero, and fewer attempts. Because it recovers more while trying less, it wins at any retry cost — even ₹0.

Honest caveat, stated in the repo itself: the bank simulator is synthetic. The LLM row is also currently blank because the configured API key has no credits — the harness refuses to fill it in with fallback numbers and pretend.

12What can go wrong — documented, not hidden

Listing your failure modes reads as senior judgment. Hiding them reads as junior.

The repo keeps a candid docs/failure_cases.md. A few examples of how the system is designed to fail gracefully:

FailureHow the system responds
LLM hallucinates / returns garbageSchema rejects it, one correction retry, then safe fallback to abandon. Logged for review.
LLM provider outageXGBoost takes over; a counter records it. No service interruption.
Process crashes before a background task runsThe event was already saved; a reconcile sweep re-runs it next tick.
Customer opts out during a deferred waitRe-checked at fire time; the scheduled retry is cancelled and audited — not sent.
Two webhooks race the same idempotency keyA UNIQUE constraint picks the winner before any API call; the loser exits cleanly.
Crash mid-Razorpay-callWrite-ahead row survives as "pending," then a sweep resolves it fail-closed. The attempt slot stays spent so nothing is double-charged.
Simulator ≠ realityStated plainly as a known limitation, with a path to swap in real bank data.

13Tech stack & how to run it

Boring, proven tools. Nothing exotic.

🐍 Backend
Python 3.11+, FastAPI, SQLAlchemy (async), Pydantic v2
🗄️ Database
PostgreSQL (events, failures, retries, ledger). Alembic for migrations.
🧠 Intelligence
Claude / GPT for the policy agent + nudges; XGBoost as the ML baseline/fallback
💳 Payments
Razorpay — webhooks in, Payment Links out (test mode moves no real money)
📊 Dashboard
Streamlit + Plotly — a 6-view operations console, password-gated
⏱️ Scheduling
In-process asyncio loop. No broker. (This is why serverless won't work — the process must stay resident.)

Run it

cp .env.example .env   # add Razorpay test keys + an LLM key
./run.sh               # build, test, migrate, start API + dashboard + tunnel

# watch it work with synthetic traffic:
python scripts/simulate_webhooks.py --count 24

run.sh does the whole job: builds a clean isolated environment, runs the linter + tests + type-checker, trains the XGBoost model if it's missing, starts the API on port 8000 and the dashboard on 8501, and opens a public tunnel — then prints the webhook URL to paste into Razorpay. It refuses to expose anything until all three checks pass.

The one-paragraph summary: This is a recovery engine for failed Indian payments. It ingests webhooks and merchant risk events, classifies each failure with a lookup table, lets a constrained AI pick one of five actions, runs that choice past eight non-negotiable safety rules, then executes it through Razorpay Payment Links — with a background scheduler handling everything deferred, and an eval harness that proves the smart policy beats a dumb fixed retry by a wide, statistically significant margin. The AI does the judging; deterministic code does everything that touches money.
