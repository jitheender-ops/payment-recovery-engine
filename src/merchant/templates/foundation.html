Step into the recovery

Failed payments are not the end of the sale.

Indian checkouts fail in the high teens, every day. The Payment Recovery Engine decides — in milliseconds — whether, when and on which rail to chase each one. Grounded in real case facts. Bounded by deterministic guardrails. Audited end to end.

Payments are complex

A failed payment carries its own story.

Insufficient funds, issuer declines, bank downtime, 3DS dropoffs, network errors — each block clears on its own clock. A fixed 3-retry schedule ignores all of them, burns attempts on hard declines, and calls the noise a strategy.

Hard declinesnever retried — the blocklist is deterministic, not a judgement call.
Bank down right nowrouted around, live, from the gateway's own downtime feed.
A promised datebuys real silence — the chase stops until the customer's own deadline.
"Band karo"honoured in any phrasing, any channel, before anything else the system does.

The engine

One case at a time, decided four ways.

A deterministic classifier reads the error code. An XGBoost policy weighs the case against simulated bank outcomes. Every rupee it can ever state is grounded in a database row, and a 12-rule guardrail sits between the decision and the money — the LLM never authorizes a charge.

Every channel answers back

Recovery is a conversation.

A Hinglish voice agent that only speaks grounded numbers. Promises with silence until the promised day. Instalment plans, B2B statements, disputes that freeze the chase — every reply becomes state the engine respects.

Voicean AI that introduces itself as one, abstains when unsure, and never invents a rupee.
Receivablesone statement per buyer, not four messages — escalated by person, not volume.
Audithash-chained case events, keyed and tamper-evident, verified on every sweep.

It has to survive its own success

Auditable at ten events, and at ten million.

The hash chain that makes every decision provable is one global sequence, so verifying it whole gets slower with every event ever written. It re-anchors instead: signed epoch checkpoints make a verification pass cost the recent history, not all of it — while a rotating pass re-reads the oldest unverified epoch from row content, so a rewrite that leaves every stored hash intact is still caught.

Verification is O(recent)checkpoints are keyed by the same secret as the chain, so deleting one cannot forge one.
Limits hold across replicasrate limits move to Redis when it is configured — a second worker no longer silently doubles every per-IP bound.
The dedup index stays hotprocessed webhook events are pruned past their redelivery window, on a bounded sweep that reports what it deleted.
