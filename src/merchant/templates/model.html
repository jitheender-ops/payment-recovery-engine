Every failed rupee gets a decision.

A payment fails and something has to choose: retry it, park it, move it to another rail, ask the customer, or stop. That choice is a model with a fixed vocabulary, wrapped in rules it cannot argue with — and it is measured against the alternative that does not bother choosing.

The classifier has no model in it.

Razorpay's five-tuple error code goes into a YAML table sorted by priority, first match wins, and out comes one of fifteen classes. A language model is asked only when nothing matches at all.

"No LLM here. A regex-solvable problem solved by an LLM is the first thing a panel will flag."

Five actions. Nothing else is representable.

The agent emits a typed action, a rail, an optional time and a reason it is made to write down. Free text is not an output the schema can hold, so "something clever" is not a thing it can return. Production runs the LLM policy and falls back to XGBoost when the provider fails — and the attempt row records who actually decided, not who was configured to.

src/guardrail/gate.py · rules.py

One signed link, on the rail most likely to clear.

The link expires, it is minted once per attempt and reused while it lives, so a customer never holds two ways to pay the same rupee. A capture through that link is the only thing counted as recovered — money that arrived some other way is counted, and never claimed.

A promise to pay buys silence until its date. If the customer authorised a UPI Autopay mandate against it, the engine collects on that date rather than waiting to be remembered.

Against a policy that does not bother choosing.

Fixed 3-retry is the honest baseline: it recovers real money by accident. Every policy runs the identical scenarios under common random numbers, so outcomes are differenced one-to-one and a difference is only called real when the interval excludes zero.
