A model in the middle, rules on both ends.

Everything before the model is a lookup; everything after it is a rule. It can be wrong — nothing reaches a payment link until the guardrail agrees.

Receive

Failed charges arrive by webhook; everything else by one signed POST /risks. Signature-checked, deduplicated, classified before anything runs.

Decide

A constrained agent picks one of five fixed actions — retry, defer, switch rail, nudge, abandon — and the reasoning is stored on every attempt.

Gate

Consent window, attempt budget, quiet-hours blackout and idempotency are re-checked the moment a chase fires. Rules, not prompts.

Recover

A signed, expiring link goes out on the rail most likely to clear. A capture through that link is the only thing counted as recovered.

Then it listens.

Sending the link is the easy half. What decides whether the money arrives is what the engine does with the reply — and the reply comes at 8pm, in Hindi, and is usually not yes.

{{ icon("phone", 20) }}

It picks up the phone, in the language the customer uses.

A Hinglish voice agent answers what the customer asks about their own case — the amount, the due date, why the charge failed. It answers only from that case's facts. Ask it something it cannot ground and it says it does not know and offers a human, because a confident invented number on a call about money is worse than no answer.

  1. “Paisa agle hafte aa jayega.”

    It takes the promise and goes quiet.

    The case stops chasing until that date — no retry, no reminder, no contact budget spent on someone who already answered. One nudge the morning it falls due. Miss the date and the promise breaks on its own clock, with an audit event, and the chase resumes where it stopped.

    promise → silence until the date

  2. “Itna ek saath nahi ho payega.”

    It splits the amount into instalments.

    A plan is a group of promises, each with its own date and its own silence. Missing one instalment breaks that instalment, not the plan. Plans are sized against what is still owed, so a customer who has already part-paid is never asked to schedule money they have sent.

    plan → one promise per instalment

  3. “Yeh invoice galat hai.”

    It stops and hands the case to you.

    A disputed invoice is a person's problem, not a chaser's. The case freezes the moment a dispute is opened and appears at the top of your console worklist. Nothing automated touches it again until you close the dispute — a chaser that argues about an invoice is how a customer is lost, not recovered.

    dispute → frozen, escalated to you

What it refuses to do.

The chase is bounded by construction, not by promise. Each of these is a rule the engine cannot talk its way around.

✕ Never chases past the consent window
Every case has a deadline. Past it the engine closes the case as expired instead of reaching out.
✕ Never messages 23:00–07:00 IST
Attempts inside the blackout are deferred to morning; the attempt budget is not burned.
✕ Never claims a payment it didn't cause
Customers who paid on their own are counted, but never attributed to the engine.
✕ Never re-mints a live link
One link per attempt, reused while it lives — a customer never holds two ways to pay the same rupee.
✕ Never loses an event
Every event is committed before it is processed, and a reconciler re-runs what a crash left behind.
✕ Never texts without an exit
One opt-out closes every open case for that customer and stops all future outreach.
